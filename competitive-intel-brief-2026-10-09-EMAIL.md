MORNINGSTAR / PITCHBOOK — HIGH — 7 Oct 2026

Morningstar Direct AI goes live with three agents built for asset managers. Morningstar relaunched its flagship Direct platform as an agentic product, live now for global users in a new browser interface. Three agents ship at launch: a Product Development Agent for fund positioning and launch decisions using flows and category data; a Distribution Agent that finds gaps in approved lists and model portfolios, shows where a fund could fit and drafts review-meeting talking points, including for portfolios holding public and private securities; and a Manager Research Agent that applies Morningstar's analyst framework to due diligence. Morningstar is also making its investment intelligence available through Claude, Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT. Scott Brown, President, Direct Platform: "AI and agents are only as valuable as the data that powers them." No pricing, no named clients.

So what: These agents are aimed at the product, distribution and manager-research teams inside the asset managers Alvaro covers, so a vendor already in the building now has agents there too; at BlackRock and Permira, pitch Moody's on credit and private-company risk, which none of the three Morningstar agents touches, and expect "we already have Morningstar in Claude" in any Claude-based demo.

MSCI — HIGH — 6 Oct 2026

MSCI and CUSIP Global Services extend CUSIP identifiers to private companies. CUSIP Global Services (CGS, operated by FactSet) and MSCI are extending CUSIPs to late-stage, venture-backed private companies, building on a CGS initiative launched in 2025. MSCI supplies reference data from its Private Company Insights database, which covers more than 2,000 private companies and over $5trn of estimated valuation, with each CUSIP carrying domicile, dividend details, asset type and MSCI PACS sector and geography classification. Scope today is US-domiciled companies with at least one share class, so this is private equity and secondaries rather than private credit. Luke Flemmer, Head of Private Assets at MSCI, called it "the common language investors need" for private market exposures. No pricing or rollout schedule.

So what: This is the second private-markets identifier standard in three weeks, after ICE's private credit IDs with Apollo on 16 Sep, so identity in private markets is splitting between camps; at Apollo and Hayfin, present Moody's Orbis/BvD entity data as the neutral layer that maps across ICE IDs and CUSIPs rather than a third standard to adopt.
https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/06/3375488/0/en/cusip-global-services-teams-with-msci-to-expand-cusip-identifiers-for-private-companies.html
https://www.msci.com/discover-msci/media-room/msci-and-cusip-global-services-bring-standardized-identifiers-to-private-markets

ANTHROPIC (SEMIANALYSIS) — MEDIUM — 5 Oct 2026 (ANALYST ESTIMATE)

SemiAnalysis: Claude subscriptions give 5x+ the value of OpenAI's, at a heavy compute subsidy. SemiAnalysis (Megalaa, Kan, Patel) estimates Anthropic subscriptions deliver roughly five times OpenAI's API-equivalent value: Claude Pro at $20 a month yields about 2.9bn tokens versus about 1bn for ChatGPT Plus. It estimates subscriptions use about 42% of Anthropic's inference compute but bring in about 10% of revenue. Separately, Artificial Analysis puts cost per completed task at API rates at $5.98 for Claude Opus 5.5 against $0.72 for GPT-6.1 Sol. These are third-party estimates, not company figures.

So what: Moody's agentic solutions run on Claude, so expect procurement at Ares and Oaktree to quote the per-task cost gap and ask what a Moody's agent costs to run per workflow; have that number ready, and treat the subsidy figure as a sign that subscription limits are more likely to tighten than loosen.

Bloomberg launches CLO Data: loan-level collateral mapped to FIGIs and entities, coming to ASKB. Bloomberg's CLO Data solution delivers normalised CLO deal structure, tranche performance, coverage tests and loan-level collateral for every loan in a CLO portfolio through Data License, with holdings mapped to FIGIs linking tranches to underlying loans and corporate entities. S&P GLOBAL RATINGS — LOW — 4 Oct 2026

S&P Global Ratings launches Vault Risk Assessment for digital-asset lending vaults. The VRA is an opinion, not a credit rating, on the relative risk that an investor's position in a blockchain lending vault is impaired, across six factors including portfolio credit quality, liquidity mismatch, curator and protocol risk. Vault deposits are cited at about $10bn in September 2026. Initial assessments will follow. Yann Le Pallec, President of S&P Global Ratings, announced it from Singapore.

So what: No European AM impact; it pairs with Moody's first stablecoin protocol rating three days later, so both agencies are moving into digital-asset credit in the same week.
