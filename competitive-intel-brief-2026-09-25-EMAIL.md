OCTUS — HIGH — 21 Sep 2026 (plus 3 Sep and 26 Aug, previously unreported)

Octus — the Permira-owned business formerly called Reorg — has put its credit intelligence inside three rival agent platforms in four weeks, each time through its own MCP connector.

On 21 September, Rogo. Octus data becomes a configurable source inside Rogo's agents: expert analysis, private company financials, capital structures, covenant data, restructuring information, credit news, debt documents, structured credit data. Workflows named are origination, underwriting, monitoring, trading, distressed, private credit and restructuring. Ben Kovacka, Head of Direct Data Services at Octus, describes users and their agents querying Octus directly to power pitchbooks and capital allocation decisions. Available to mutual customers holding an Octus licence.

On 3 September, Harvey. Same intelligence embedded in Harvey's agentic legal platform via the Octus MCP Connector — covenants, private company financials, a US court filings database, market news, creditor dynamics, advisor relationship histories to follow. Aimed at AmLaw firms and in-house teams doing restructuring and leveraged finance work.

On 26 August, Perplexity. Octus live inside Perplexity's answer engine and Computer agent platform, again via the MCP connector, with permissioned access to proprietary intelligence not indexed on the open web.

Prior editions missed the Harvey and Perplexity deals. Read as one move, this is a company that has decided its distribution strategy is to be the credit layer inside whatever agent the client already uses.

So what: this is the exact play Moody's Credit MCP was built for, run from a credit-native starting point and at three times the pace. And because Permira owns Octus, the Permira conversation now has a competitor's own portfolio company as the reference architecture in the room. Lead there with ratings depth and issuer coverage. Do not lead with connector availability — you will be told they already own the connector.

ICE AND APOLLO — HIGH — 16 Sep 2026

ICE launched a private credit reference data service built on ICE IDs — permanent deal-level identifiers assigned at origination that stay attached through amendments, credit events and repayment. ICE released the identifiers in July 2026; this is the data service on top, converting key loan terms into standardised fields delivered by desktop, file, cloud and API. Access is entitlement-gated, so only counterparties authorised on a deal see its data.

Apollo is the founding data contributor, with more than 5,000 deals and over $1.3trn of notional. Chris Edmonds, President of ICE Fixed Income and Data Services, tied it to 25 years working with partners like Apollo. Eric Needleman, Partner and Head of Apollo Capital Solutions, called standardisation an essential building block. The service sits on ICE infrastructure covering 35m-plus instruments across 210-plus markets. Named future modules: deal calculators, relative-value analytics, secondary trading, performance indices. No pricing disclosed.

So what: Apollo, a named target account, has put its own origination book behind a competing identifier standard for private credit. Any Moody's private-credit surveillance or EDF-X pitch into Apollo now has to reconcile with ICE IDs rather than assume a greenfield. The honest opening line is that identifiers are plumbing and default probability and recovery judgement are not.

ROGO — HIGH — 10 Sep 2026 (pre-window, previously unreported)

Rogo took strategic investment from Barclays, BNP Paribas via Opera Tech Ventures, Citi Ventures, MUFG Innovation Partners and Société Générale, plus Sixth Street co-founder Josh Easterly, joining existing backers J.P. Morgan Growth Equity Partners and Truist Ventures. Amount and valuation undisclosed. The stated use of capital is expansion across EMEA and APAC, scaling regional operations to hundreds of employees in the coming months. Gabe Stengel: AI for this industry "has shifted from experiment to infrastructure."

It sits alongside a busy supply-side month — the Octus partnership above, a Datasite partnership on 8 September, and the acquisition of Arvo, an AI meeting assistant for financial institutions. The 12 Sep edition logged Rogo as nothing-net-new; this was live two days earlier and was missed.

So what: five banks your alternative-manager accounts deal with daily are now shareholders in an AI-native research agent that is about to put hundreds of people into EMEA. Rogo will be in the room at Permira, ICG and Hayfin on a Barclays or SocGen introduction well before it is on ours.

ANTHROPIC — MEDIUM — 14–15 Sep 2026

Anthropic launched Claude for Financial Advisors at the Future Proof Festival, a vertical product for advisers and RIAs designed to integrate with custodians, portfolio platforms, CRMs and planning tools. BlackRock, Schwab and Vanguard are named among connected providers. Peter Nolan, head of asset and wealth management, frames it as a streamlined advisor workflow. Dynasty Financial Partners simultaneously launched Dynasty AI on Claude models. Pricing not disclosed.

So what: this is wealth, not institutional AM, so it moves no live deal. It matters as evidence that Anthropic is verticalising by buyer segment rather than by data domain, which is the argument for getting Moody's content named inside whatever institutional equivalent lands next rather than waiting to appear as one connector on someone else's list.

BLACKROCK ALADDIN — MEDIUM — 24 Sep 2026

Cameron Skinner, global head of solutions engineering for Aladdin, told a panel that Aladdin is putting considerable effort into semantic foundations before deploying agentic capabilities, and that the distinction between AI-enabled and AI-native will drive both implementation timelines and when value lands. The WatersTechnology article is paywalled; the readable section names no client deployments and does not mention eFront, Preqin or Aladdin Copilot, so treat anything beyond the headline claim as unconfirmed.

So what: the largest platform in the category is publicly saying agents are gated on data semantics. That is the cleanest third-party endorsement available for the line that an agent is only as good as the credit data underneath it — use it verbatim with anyone at Ares or HPS still treating the agent layer as the project.

S&P GLOBAL — MEDIUM — 14 and 17 Sep 2026

On 17 September S&P agreed to acquire OpenZeppelin, a blockchain security firm with 900-plus security engagements whose contract library underpins infrastructure securing roughly $37trn of transferred value. Terms undisclosed, not expected to be financially material. Yann Le Pallec framed it as bringing trusted data, benchmarks and transparent risk assessment to markets moving onchain. On 14 September S&P led a strategic investment in Kaiko, the crypto market data firm, following the S&P Kaiko Digital Asset Indices launch on 1 September.

So what: no read-across to European AM credit, but a useful non-snide framing for any account weighing a Capital IQ renewal against the reported separation — S&P's incremental capital is going into digital assets while its structure is publicly in question, and the Moody's platform stays whole.
