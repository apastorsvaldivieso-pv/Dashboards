LSEG / OCTUS — HIGH — UPDATE — talks previously covered 16 Oct — 22 Oct 2026

LSEG signs the Octus deal: $3.535bn cash, Octus revenue above $300m at a ~40% margin, closing H1 2027. New since last week's talks item: a signed agreement. LSEG pays $3.535bn in cash, financed with external debt, to Permira-advised funds and Octus management (including founder Kent Collier). Octus expects 2026 revenue above $300m with strong double-digit growth and an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 40%; it has about 750 customers, 40,000+ users and 900+ staff. Completion is expected in H1 2027, subject to regulators. Octus joins Data & Analytics, with Collier reporting to co-heads Gianluca Biagini and Ron Lefferts. Stated plan: sell Octus across LSEG's footprint and build combined products with Workspace, LPC, Yield Book, PRS and Tradeweb, working toward one data model, valuation approach and portfolio tools across public and private credit. EPS-neutral in the first full year, accretive after. No synergy figure disclosed.

So what: The competitor at Ares, HPS, Oaktree, Hayfin and Permira is now LSEG, which owns both the private credit news and the LPC loan data and plans to sell them as one product; the Moody's answer in those rooms is still ratings, EDF-X PDs and the Allvue-based Private Credit Model as the risk view Octus does not give, and with closing not due until H1 2027 there is a window to lock renewals before LSEG starts bundling.

S&P GLOBAL MARKET INTELLIGENCE — HIGH — 22 Oct 2026

iLEVEL gets an agent, an MCP server for Claude and Copilot, and private credit covenant monitoring. S&P added AI to iLEVEL, its portfolio monitoring platform used by 700+ private markets firms: a built-in iLEVEL Agent, AI data ingestion and natural-language search. A new MCP server lets clients connect iLEVEL data to third-party AI tools such as Copilot and Claude with existing permissions kept. For private credit, iLEVEL now tests covenant compliance against credit agreement terms and flags deterioration and emerging risks. Other additions: tighter integration with S&P Private Market Valuations (software or outsourced), more contributed GP performance data across private credit, real assets and PE, and links to Capital IQ, WSO and CLM Pro. No clients or availability dates disclosed. Kelly McGeehan, Head of Markets & Regulatory, Enterprise Solutions, announced it.

So what: This is the most direct answer yet to the Moody's EDF-X Private Credit Model with Allvue (1 Oct): S&P now offers covenant and deterioration monitoring inside the system many private credit managers already use to collect borrower data, so expect 'we already have that in iLEVEL' at any iLEVEL-using lender. The reply is that a covenant check shows a breach once it has happened, while Moody's estimates the probability of credit events (waivers, PIK) before they happen.

INDUSTRY (AI DATA LICENSING) — HIGH — 20 Oct 2026

Substantive Research: AI charges in 2026 vendor proposals ran $30k–$1m, and AI licensing could add up to 50% to some 2027 contracts. Substantive Research (with BCG Expand and TRG Screen) found that 12 major data vendors have told clients of 2027 pricing changes on seven-figure agreements. AI-related charges in 2026 proposals ranged from $30,000 to $1m, some opening asks sought increases of up to 300%, and changes to AI licensing could lift some institutional market data agreements by as much as 50% in 2027. BCG Expand expects market data spend to rise another 7% in 2026. On research, a survey of 50 large asset managers (about $20trn AUM) found little evidence that brokers or independent providers have been paid extra for delivering content into clients' AI systems. Vendors are not named. CEO Mike Carrodus: "You're not seeing any emerging standardisation of approach."

So what: Market data and procurement teams at European asset managers now have numbers to resist AI surcharges, which will come up in every Moody's MCP or agentic pricing talk during the Q4 renewal season; make sure the Moody's AI licence is clearly scoped and justified by usage before anyone at BlackRock or Apollo quotes the 50% figure back.

MSCI — MEDIUM — 20 Oct 2026

Q3: revenue +12.4%, Private Capital Solutions run rate +19%, EMEA net new sales +74%, 'AI-first' push. MSCI's Q3 revenue rose 12.4%, with asset-based fees up 25% and subscriptions up 8%; subscription run rate +9% on nearly $55m of recurring net new sales, retention 95%. Private Capital Solutions subscription run rate growth accelerated to 19%; real assets grew 2%. LTM recurring net new subscription sales rose 26%, with EMEA up 74%. Analytics net new sales rose 63%. Asset manager subscription run rate grew about 6%, slower than hedge funds (about 18%) and asset owners (12%+). Management described an AI-first push across products, retention and pricing, citing a custom model service, specialised datasets and an AI-native private markets diligence tool (the Vantager acquisition). CEO Henry Fernandez said there will be no margin expansion as MSCI prioritises growth.

So what: MSCI is winning new business in EMEA and growing its private capital line at 19%, so expect it in LP-side and private markets data conversations, especially at asset owners and fund-of-funds; its weaker growth with asset managers (about 6%) suggests the EMEA alternative managers on Alvaro's list are not where it is gaining.

LSEG — MEDIUM — 22 Oct 2026

Q3 trading update: organic growth 8.8%, guidance raised to at least 8%, AI Search users nearly tripled to 47,000. Published with the Octus deal. Q3 revenue excluding recoveries was £2.41bn, up 8.8% organically, ahead of consensus (£2.38bn per MarketScreener). Data & Analytics grew 5.2%; subscription businesses accelerated to 6.6%. Full-year guidance raised for the second time: organic growth of at least 8% (from 7–7.5%), 100–120bp EBITDA margin expansion. Shares rose about 7–8% intraday (MarketScreener quote; treat as indicative). Bloomberg framed the day as LSEG pushing back on AI-disruption fears.

So what: LSEG now has figures to show its data is being used inside AI tools, which strengthens its pitch to the buy-side as an AI data supplier; have Moody's own MCP adoption figures from this week's Q3 call (paying MCP customers doubled) ready as the equivalent.

ANTHROPIC — MEDIUM — 22 Oct 2026

Anthropic donates the Agent Skills standard to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation. Anthropic has handed the Agent Skills open standard (with its name, GitHub organisation and agentskills.io domain) to the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, where it already placed MCP. More than 70 agent platforms have adopted it, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code; Anthropic cites 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installs. Engineers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and others will maintain it. No financial firms named. Source is Anthropic's own announcement.

So what: Moody's AI Skills (17 Jun) use this SKILL.md format, so a vendor-neutral standard lets Alvaro say the same Moody's skills will run in Claude, Copilot or ChatGPT; that answers the lock-in question risk and architecture teams ask at BlackRock and Apollo.
