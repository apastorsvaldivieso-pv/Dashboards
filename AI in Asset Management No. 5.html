Key stories

Moody's research

Moody's: AI issuance pushes the US investment grade maturity wall to a record

Moody's Ratings published research showing a record $1.56trn of US investment grade bonds coming due in 2027 to 2031, up 8% on last year's study. Heavy issuance by technology and services companies to fund AI infrastructure played a significant role.

Why it mattersThe AI build-out is now part of the refinancing calendar our clients' investment grade portfolios have to watch.

Moody's research

Moody's on a GPU financing vehicle rated Baa2

Moody's published a credit update on Lambda Compute II, which it rated Baa2 in August, a vehicle that finances and operates 10,368 Nvidia GPUs in a data centre in Québec. Lambda is a GPU cloud provider, and the vehicle's cash flows rest on a four-year take-or-pay contract with Nvidia itself.

Why it mattersGPU-backed debt is becoming a rated asset class, and these are the deals our clients are lending into (see the lending item below).

IMF

IMF: an AI-driven market correction is one of the three big risks

The IMF's Global Financial Stability Report names an AI-driven market correction as one of three salient near-term risks. Hyperscalers' share of global investment grade bond issuance has risen to 10%, from less than 1%. It also flags circular deals, where one company invests in another that buys its products and services: 14 since 2023, with about $300bn of equity and $650bn of debt in exchange for $1.4trn of future revenue commitments.

Why it mattersThe regulator and our own research above point at the same exposure, AI debt sitting in our clients' bond and loan books.

Markets · 14–18 Oct

AI debt jitters spread from CoreWeave to Oracle, Blue Owl and Nscale

CoreWeave, an AI cloud provider that has funded its growth mostly with debt, saw its bond yields briefly touch 15% on 14 Oct. Its shares fell more than 15% that day, with Oracle down 8.8% and Dell 11.3%, SiliconANGLE reported. Its five-year credit default swaps (the cost of insuring its debt against default) then reached a record high of about 1,195 basis points, The Motley Fool reported.

The NYT reported that some debt on Oracle's data centres has traded around 90 cents on the dollar, and some on Blue Owl's New Mexico project at about 80 cents. And Microsoft pulled out of anchoring Nscale's first UK data centre, in Essex, weeks before the AI cloud company's planned IPO, TNW reported.

Why it mattersThe IMF's warning is already showing up in prices, and now in Europe too. Moody's MCP shows this risk from several angles in one place: ratings, default probabilities, research and who owns whom.

OpenAI

OpenAI: many MCP connectors weren't good enough, so it licensed the data

At AI Engineer NYC, a conference for engineers building AI, OpenAI said it looked at existing MCP connectors for financial data and found many had problems with speed, errors or search quality. So it licensed a lot of the data directly and built its own search.

Why it mattersOpenAI goes around MCP connectors that fail on speed and answer quality. That is where we stand out: TDR Capital, which is testing our MCP, compared it with nine others on quality and accuracy, and ours came out on top.

Anthropic

Anthropic: five layers for building finance agents at scale

At the same conference, Anthropic set out one reusable foundation instead of hand-building each agent: MCP connectors, skills, agent identity and permissions, approval steps and evaluations. MCP gives agents access to tools and data. Skills tell them how the business does the work: procedures written by the firm's experts and kept up to date, like a playbook the agent follows.

Why it mattersAnthropic puts MCP at the base of every finance agent, as the layer that connects it to data and tools. That is exactly where our MCP sits, ready to plug Moody's content into the agents our clients build.

Competition

LSEG

LSEG in talks to buy Octus, as Octus goes live in Hebbia

LSEG confirmed it is in talks with Permira to buy Octus, a credit intelligence and data provider, for about $3.5bn. No final agreement has been reached. The same day, Hebbia (an AI platform for investment research) added Octus through Octus's MCP connector.

Why it mattersCompetition on our home ground getting closer. LSEG would get private credit data that is already wired into the AI tools our clients use.

Bloomberg

Bloomberg opens ASKB to every Terminal user

Bloomberg made ASKB generally available, its AI assistant on the Terminal. Once switched on, it can also answer from the client's own chats, watchlists and uploaded files, citing the source. Shawn Edwards, Bloomberg's CTO: "This isn't Bloomberg building on AI. It's AI built on Bloomberg."

Why it mattersMost of our clients already have Terminals, so ASKB will be on many desks. But clients have told us Bloomberg has been slow to react, and what they keep asking for is an MCP connection into their own AI tools, not AI inside the Terminal.

S&P Global

S&P puts Visible Alpha estimates into its AI Data Portal

S&P Global added Visible Alpha's consensus estimates, from more than 250 brokers, to its AI Data Portal through MCP. Queries across several datasets come later this year.

Why it mattersS&P keeps adding data to its AI portal, so clients will compare what each vendor's MCP covers.

What asset managers are doing

Apollo · Blackstone

Apollo and Blackstone step into the AI labs' financing

Semafor reported that Apollo is anchoring Volta's $5bn debt deal for a data centre in Norway with a $1.5bn order. Semafor put the pricing at 11% or more, and the rate falls if Anthropic, which is to buy much of the capacity, gets an investment grade rating. The FT then reported that Anthropic offered Blackstone a coupon above 10% to anchor $9bn of the junior tranche in a $60bn package that funds its lease of Google's TPU chips. It is an offer, not a closed deal.

Why it mattersPrivate credit is being asked to take on AI lab risk, and the coupon can depend on one lab's credit rating. That is exposure clients will want to map (the Lambda, IMF and AI debt items above).

Blackstone

Blackstone bets big on AI, outside and inside

Bloomberg reported that Jon Gray, Blackstone's president, calls its AI investments "the single most important thing" underway at the firm. Including debt and projects under construction, its data centre investments reached $200bn as of July, up from $55bn in June 2024. Inside the firm, nearly every deal at the investment committee now comes with two memos, one from the deal team and one from an AI model trained on more than 14 years of internal deal memos and debates. The model has no vote.

Why it mattersThe biggest alternative manager is betting on AI both as an investor in the build-out and as a user inside its investment committee, with people still making the call (see the concept below).

AI Engineer NYC

At AI Engineer NYC, the buy side showed how it builds agents

  • Apollo: Jack McDonald, an Apollo principal and COO of Redding Ridge, a CLO manager, said agents built with Rowspace now prepare a draft CLO portfolio in minutes, work that took a portfolio manager days in Excel. The PM still refines the last part.
  • Bridgewater: Vault holds more than 100 million documents for its investors and agents. Better search lifted the share of right documents in the top ten results from about 60% to roughly 90 to 95%. Separately, a model Bridgewater trained with UIUC beat frontier models on a database-query test at 15% of their cost.
  • Citadel Securities: COO João Wenzel described a world model of the firm, built from Slack, decks and meetings, that flags projects that look healthy but aren't. In a backtest, flagged green projects turned yellow or red almost twice as often as the rest. His line: "Your company has no model of itself."

Why it mattersThe big funds own the harness (the software around the model, last week's concept) and the data strategy, and they test the sources they plug in. Our content has to work inside the stack they build.

Benefit Street Partners

Benefit Street: a PM builds his own credit tools

In an OpenAI case study, Anant Kumar, a portfolio manager at Benefit Street Partners (a credit manager), built a tool that compares how BDCs value the same loan, and a credit agreement analyser that flags clauses that shift value away from lenders. He built both himself, with help from OpenAI's Codex and GPT-6 Astra. The analyser does a first pass in about 20 minutes, then a person reviews it for an hour or more.

Why it mattersA PM can now build a credit tool on public filings in a day. Our case is the data public filings don't have.

Concept of the week

Human in the loop

Blackstone's AI memo has no vote, and Anthropic's agents propose changes that a person approves. Human in the loop (HITL) is a system or process in which a human actively takes part in running, monitoring or making decisions inside an automated system.

Think of autopilot: the plane flies itself most of the way, but a pilot sits in the seat and takes over for the moments that matter.

You'll hear this from clients when they say the agent can draft the memo, but a person signs it off.
