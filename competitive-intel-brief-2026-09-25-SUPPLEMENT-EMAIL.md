FACTSET — MEDIUM — 22 Sep 2026 (missed by this morning's edition)

FactSet has acquired BCC Group International GmbH (bccg), a Germany-based firm founded over twenty years ago that builds vendor-neutral market data distribution technology. Its flagship ONE Platform lets institutions ingest, entitle and distribute market data across hybrid and cloud environments — AWS, Azure and Google Cloud. It folds into FactSet's Real-Time Solution Suite.

No price or terms disclosed; FactSet says it is not expected to be material to financial results. What FactSet gains is a governed open ecosystem for combining exchange feeds with proprietary data, API-first interoperable components, flexible cloud, on-premises and hybrid deployment, and a managed service with KPI monitoring. Brian Herte, Global Head of Market Data and Trading at FactSet, says clients "don't just want another data vendor" but an open technology partner. Mauricio Gonzalez Evans, CEO of BCC Group, frames the combination as giving institutions "a genuinely source-neutral alternative." The release names 24-hour trading, AI-changed workflows and regulatory complexity as drivers, but gives no AI product detail and no private-markets application.

So what: whoever owns entitlement and distribution owns the commercial relationship, and FactSet has just bought the right to sit as the source-neutral pipe carrying other people's content — potentially ours. Expect the "open architecture, we are not locked to one vendor" line in any enterprise data conversation at platform-scale accounts running hybrid cloud, and answer it on entitlement economics and who holds the client relationship, not on content breadth.

FACTSET — MEDIUM — 25 Sep 2026 (landed after this morning's edition)

FactSet has appointed Marcel Prins to its Board of Directors. Prins has 25-plus years in financial services technology and operations: Chief Operating Officer and director of Robeco from 2022 to 2026, before that COO and director of APG Asset Management and Chief Digital Officer of APG Group, with earlier roles at ABN AMRO Group, Fortis Bank and DCE International. Board Chair Malcolm Frank cited his technology and asset management experience; FactSet tied the appointment to putting "trusted financial data and advanced AI capabilities to work" for clients. It lands three days before FactSet's Q4 FY2026 print on 30 September.

So what: FactSet has put a Dutch institutional-AM operating executive — the precise COO and CDO profile that signs off data and AI spend at a Robeco or an APG — inside its own boardroom, which is a deliberate signal to European institutional buyers that FactSet understands their operating model. Not a deal event, but any "FactSet is a US sell-side tool" framing in a European AM room is now out of date and should be dropped.

NASDAQ PRIVATE MARKET — LOW — 17 Sep 2026 (missed by this morning's edition)

Nasdaq Private Market launched PAM, an AI agent inside its iOS app, in public beta. It answers questions on companies and funds, surfaces opportunities against stated investor goals, explains deal mechanics and executes transactions, drawing on NPM's proprietary private-company data — pricing history, transaction activity, company fundamentals. Aimed at accredited investors; NPM simultaneously cut minimums on certain single-asset funds to $5,000. CEO Tom Callahan describes turning a process that needed specialised knowledge and paperwork "into a conversation." No named clients, no pricing beyond the minimum, functionality rolling out in phases.

So what: accredited-retail access, not institutional AM, so no read-across to any account in the book — its only use is as evidence that a conversational private-markets agent now exists at a $5,000 ticket, which is another reason no Moody's pitch should spend a slide proving we have an agent.

ANTHROPIC — MEDIUM — 22 Sep 2026 (missed by the morning edition)

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5. List price is $4 / $20 per million input/output tokens, down from $5 / $25 for Opus 5, and cache reads drop 60% to $0.20. Anthropic says it costs about 40% less to run than Opus 5 on typical workloads and is about 30% faster. It is available through the API, AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and claude.ai, with Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 expected in the coming weeks. Financial-services customers quoted at launch: Rogo (token efficiency on financial analysis), Hebbia (86.6% coverage on its finance workflow evals), Walleye Capital, Chicago Trading Company and Box AI. The cost and speed figures are Anthropic's own.

So what: the model under the Moody's × Anthropic integration just got about 40% cheaper, which weakens the token-cost objection at BlackRock, Apollo and Ares. But Rogo and Hebbia are on the launch page and pay the same lower price. The model gives no one an edge; the licensed credit content in the answer is what stays ours.
https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5

OPENAI — MEDIUM — 22 Sep 2026 (missed by the morning edition)

OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, the smaller tiers below GPT-6 Astra (covered 4 Sep). Pricing is half that of the GPT-5.6 Sol and Luna: Sol at $2 / $10 per million tokens and Luna at $0.10 / $0.50, per trackers and trade press. OpenAI says Sol gives "Astra-level reliability at much lower cost." Sol is live in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. ChatGPT Work is also where ChatGPT for Financial Services runs (covered 12 Sep). No finance customers were named.

So what: both frontier labs cut prices on the same day, so the cost of running a model is falling fast. That makes "we're integrated with Claude" worth less as a headline. Lead with data rights and coverage, which don't get cheaper when the model does.
https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/openai-launches-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/
