One story dominated the week and it is the one that matters most. On 10 September OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services, and in doing so published the first public tiering of financial data vendors inside a frontier-model workspace. Moody's is in it — but in the entitlement tier, not the bundled tier. PitchBook, Daloopa, LSEG News, Crunchbase and Fiscal.ai are bundled: a user gets them without buying anything extra. That distinction is the whole item, and it is the one to walk into next week understanding.

Everything else is secondary but usable: no EU data-residency or DORA answer in the launch (a live procurement wedge), SIX quietly licensing European exchange data for AI-model training "without onerous restrictions", and a Nasdaq study of 406 product executives showing the same adoption-versus-depth gap the Clearwater research showed a fortnight ago. Moody's itself announced nothing of its own in the window.

High — changes what to say in the room

HIGH 10 Sep 2026
OpenAI

ChatGPT for Financial Services launches — and publishes a vendor tier list

OpenAI launched an industry build of ChatGPT for investment banking and equity research, running GPT-6 Astra, with Morgan Stanley and Evercore as design partners. It does research across sources with line-item citations, builds models and spreadsheets, and generates decks against firm templates, with role-based access, information barriers, workspace retention controls and compliance-log exports; business data is not used for training by default. The commercially significant part is how data is tiered. Bundled premium data (included, no separate subscription): Daloopa, PitchBook, LSEG News, Crunchbase, Fiscal.ai. Entitlement integrations (shared sign-in against what the firm already licenses): S&P Capital IQ, LSEG, MSCI, Dow Jones Factiva and Moody's. Connectors (50+): FactSet, Preqin, Datasite, Box, Intapp. Available to eligible financial institutions; pricing and seat minimums undisclosed. VentureBeat additionally describes S&P Global and FactSet as having "optimised connectors" — a middle rung OpenAI's own page does not name.

So what for Alvaro Moody's content is reachable inside the workspace that Apollo, Ares and HPS analysts will actually be sitting in, which is the right side of the argument — but being in the entitlement tier while PitchBook is bundled means an analyst meets PitchBook for free and meets Moody's only if someone has already paid, so the renewal conversation at every one of those accounts now has an "is this licence still earning its seat" framing it did not have last week; get ahead of it by asking, in the next call at each, whether they are piloting ChatGPT for Financial Services and whether their Moody's entitlement has been wired into it, because an unwired entitlement is an invisible one.
Sources: OpenAI — Introducing ChatGPT for Financial Services · VentureBeat · TechRepublic
HIGH 10 Sep 2026
Morningstar / PitchBook

PitchBook's private-markets firmographics go natively into ChatGPT — no connector to switch on

PitchBook confirmed its expanded firmographic dataset — companies, investors and funds, the material used for sourcing and diligence — is indexed inside ChatGPT for Financial Services and queryable in natural language with citations, available immediately through the Essential connector to any team already on the product and requiring no separate connector activation. Tom Van Buskirk, EVP of Technology and Engineering at PitchBook, and Nick Turley, VP Product at OpenAI, on the record.

So what for Alvaro This is the fourth distribution route for private-markets fund and investor data in six weeks — Aladdin, LSEG Workspace, Rogo's Felix and now ChatGPT natively — and it is the first that costs the user nothing and needs no procurement step, which finishes the argument the 4 Sep edition called over: at Permira, Hayfin, ICG and Ares stop defending private-markets breadth entirely and put the whole weight on issuer- and instrument-level credit (ratings, PDs, recovery, sector benchmarks) that no bundled firmographic dataset carries.
Sources: PitchBook / Yahoo Finance · Investing News Network

Medium — shifts positioning

MEDIUM 11 Sep 2026
OpenAI — European angle

The launch says nothing about EU data residency or DORA

The Next Web flagged that the announcement names no European hosting region, no EU data-residency commitment and no DORA alignment — leaving European buyers to establish, in procurement, where data is stored, which subcontractors touch it, how the ICT-provider register and contractual audit and subcontracting terms are satisfied, what the exit strategy is, and how resilience testing is handled. No European customer is named anywhere in the launch; the two design partners are US.

So what for Alvaro This is the cleanest wedge available against an OpenAI-first story at a European manager, and it is an argument Moody's can make without attacking the product: a DORA-regulated firm cannot put an ICT dependency into a critical research workflow without register entries, subcontracting terms and an exit plan, so ask the AI lead at Apollo, Ares and HPS who has signed off the DORA register entry for ChatGPT for Financial Services — the honest answer is usually nobody yet, and that is the window in which the licensed-content conversation stays open.
Source: The Next Web (11 Sep)
MEDIUM 8 Sep 2026
SIX

SIX launches Market Signal — European exchange data licensed for AI-model training "without onerous restrictions"

SIX launched Market Signal, an API-based marketplace for real-time and historical European cash-equity data: 1BBO best bid/offer across SIX Swiss Exchange, BME in Spain and sixteen further European markets via Aquis. Positioned explicitly as an affordable alternative to direct feeds, opened beyond trading desks to wealth management, benchmarking and compliance, and — the part that matters — licensed for training trading models and AI agents without the usual restrictions. Institutional access is live now, Swiss retail is open, EU and UK expansion is planned. Santiago Ximenez, Head Data & Connectivity at SIX, on the record.

So what for Alvaro A European exchange group has just set a permissive public precedent on the single commercial question live in every one of our AI conversations — whether licensed data may be used to train or ground models — and buyers will start citing it, so expect "SIX lets us train on it, why can't we" at Permira, Hayfin and ICG and have the distinction ready: exchange price data is a commodity feed priced for volume, ratings and credit assessments are opinions carrying liability and attribution obligations, which is why the terms differ.
Sources: SIX media release (8 Sep) · WatersTechnology
MEDIUM 11 Sep 2026
Nasdaq

Nasdaq's Voice of the Issuer study: 80% of asset managers use AI in product development, only 26% at any depth

Nasdaq's 2026 Global Voice of the Issuer study, run with Escalent, surveyed 406 senior investment-product professionals — 356 asset managers and 50 insurers — at firms above $25bn AUM across seven countries. 80% use AI somewhere in product development globally (84% in Asia-Pacific), but only 26% deploy it across multiple stages of the process. 82% of Americas firms rank private markets in their top five demand areas; 71% say breaking through in crowded markets is getting harder and 66% say new products struggle to gather meaningful assets; 55% name brand credibility as the leading differentiator.

So what for Alvaro The 80-versus-26 gap is the same shape as the Clearwater 79/56 accuracy gap already in play and it generalises the frame beyond risk into product development — near-universal adoption, almost no depth — so pair the two when opening at Apollo, Ares and HPS: the constraint on depth is not model access, which everyone now has for free inside ChatGPT, but whether the content underneath will survive an investment committee.
Source: InvestmentNews on the Nasdaq / Escalent study (11 Sep)

What this changes for next week

The single job next week is to find out, account by account, whether Apollo, Ares, HPS and BlackRock are piloting ChatGPT for Financial Services and whether anyone has wired the Moody's entitlement into it, because an entitlement nobody has connected looks exactly like a licence nobody needs when renewal comes round. Use the DORA and EU data-residency gap as the way into that conversation rather than as an attack — no European manager can put an unregistered ICT dependency into a research workflow, and asking who has signed the register entry is a question their AI lead will not have an answer to. Drop private-markets breadth from the Permira, Hayfin, ICG and Ares conversations entirely now that PitchBook firmographics arrive free and pre-indexed, and put the weight on issuer- and instrument-level credit instead. Expect "SIX licenses its data for AI training, why don't you" and answer on the difference between a commodity price feed and a liability-bearing credit opinion rather than on policy. Internally, the distribution story — Moody's content live in Microsoft 365 Copilot, Gemini Enterprise and now ChatGPT for Financial Services — is stronger than anything Moody's has said publicly about it, and someone should be told that.
