Key stories

Moody's · Q3 call

Moody's: MCP users more than quadrupled in three months

On the Q3 call, Rob Fauber said MCP users more than quadrupled and usage grew more than tenfold in the last three months. Customers paying for MCP access more than doubled in the quarter, from a small base. He also said Moody's is thinking about elements of consumption-based pricing (paying for what you use), with more to come early in 2027.

Why it mattersMCP is growing fast, and pricing is moving towards usage (see Substantive Research below).

Substantive Research

AI licensing could add up to 50% to some 2027 data contracts

A study found that 12 major data vendors have told clients about 2027 pricing changes, and AI-related charges in 2026 proposals ranged from $30,000 to $1m. Ratings, index data, terminals and ESG are all affected, driven by MCP connections, feeds into LLMs and firm-wide AI access.

Why it mattersPaying for AI access is becoming standard, which helps our MCP pricing conversation.

Moody's research

Moody's: US life insurers' reported AI holdings are small, but concentration is rising

Moody's Ratings found that US life insurers held about $39.5bn of data centre debt at the end of Q2, up 34% since year-end, plus $32.2bn of hyperscaler debt, about 6.6% of what the hyperscalers owe. Moody's says these reported holdings, under 2% of invested assets, understate the real exposure, because more comes through guarantees, backstops and leases.

The same week, Moody's published on how Big Tech uses its credit strength to back AI labs and neoclouds (AI cloud providers), and its methodology for rating data centre securitisations.

Why it mattersInsurance money is funding the AI build-out, and this research shows where the concentration sits.

Bloomberg

AI chip debt is heading for $1trn

Bloomberg reported that debt backed by AI chips is on track to become a trillion-dollar market. One example is Big Sky, a $35bn deal by Apollo and Blackstone for Anthropic: a special vehicle buys the chips and leases them to Anthropic, and Broadcom's backstop earned the senior tranches an investment grade rating. Insurance money is part of it: on its Q3 call, Blackstone said insurance clients get about 150 basis points over comparably rated public credit on what it originates for them.

Why it mattersClients need data to monitor their exposure across the whole AI debt stack, from the labs and neoclouds to the chipmakers and hyperscalers behind them, the same need as the insurer research above.

Competition

LSEG

LSEG agrees to buy Octus for $3.535bn

LSEG has agreed to buy Octus, a credit intelligence and data provider, from Permira, subject to regulatory approval. LSEG says Octus brings a strong footprint with hedge funds and alternative managers, where LSEG has historically been under-penetrated. In its Q3 update, LSEG also said more than two-thirds of its top 50 Data & Analytics customers have connected to its MCP servers.

Why it mattersLSEG is buying private credit data that is embedded in our clients' workflows.

S&P Global

S&P adds an MCP server and an agent to iLEVEL

S&P expanded iLEVEL, its private markets platform for portfolio monitoring and valuations, used by more than 700 firms. Clients can now connect iLEVEL data to Copilot and Claude through an MCP server. For private credit, iLEVEL supports covenant monitoring against credit agreement terms.

Why it mattersS&P is taking MCP into private credit portfolio monitoring, so expect it in conversations with lenders.

MSCI · Q3 call

MSCI: connectors built, but most clients only have Copilot

On MSCI's Q3 call, its Chief Product Officer said MSCI has built connectors for all the main LLM providers, but most of its clients only have access to Microsoft Copilot.

Why it mattersMSCI sees Copilot, while we see more Claude in our client conversations, so it is a useful different perspective. Either way, our data has to reach the tool the clients use.

PitchBook

PitchBook adds Skills, scheduled agents and a Data MCP

On 15 Oct, PitchBook launched Skills for market intelligence, deal sourcing and credit research, and scheduled agents that run routine work such as daily watch-list briefings. A new PitchBook Data MCP brings part of its data into clients' own AI tools. It says use of its Premium Connector is up nearly 2,000% this year.

Why it mattersThe market seems to be moving to agents and workflows, something we need to consider.

What asset managers are doing

Private credit

Private credit managers: 96% use AI, and pricing is a worry

Alternative Credit Investor reported that 96% of private credit managers now use AI to support their investment strategies. Veld Capital, a €2.7bn asset-backed credit specialist, uses generative AI for portfolio monitoring, deal screening and first-pass underwriting. Its COO and CFO flagged AI pricing as a potential difficulty for smaller firms. Allvue's head of product for credit said larger firms are more cautious because they don't want to share their data with an LLM.

Why it mattersThis matches what we see in the market. The mid-market uses AI tools directly, while large managers keep their data in-house and build on their own infrastructure, such as Snowflake.

Trading firms

Jane Street, HRT and XTX race for AI compute

Bloomberg reported that trading firms now compete on compute. Jane Street has a $1bn stake in CoreWeave plus a $6bn compute pledge, HRT spends $1bn a year on AI, and XTX is building a €1bn data centre in Finland.

Why it mattersSome trading firms are now big customers of, and investors in, the AI clouds whose debt sits in credit portfolios (see the chip debt item above).

Private equity

Hg and Anthropic to create a European AI consultancy

The FT reported that Anthropic and Hg, the London private equity firm, agreed to create an independent company that embeds consultants and engineers in European businesses to deploy AI. It will have hubs in London and Berlin and launch by the end of the year.

Why it mattersMore Claude builders inside European portfolio companies, and every agent they build needs data it can trust.

Concept of the week

Agents, subagents and skills

PitchBook now sells skills and scheduled agents (see above). Skills are reusable instructions, knowledge or workflows an agent loads when it needs them, while subagents are isolated workers that do one part of the job in their own context and return only a summary.

Think of a deal team: the lead (the agent) sends juniors to research separate questions (subagents), each comes back with a one-page summary, and everyone follows the firm's credit memo template (a skill).

You'll hear this from clients when they ask for a skill for their IC memo, or an agent that checks covenants across the portfolio.
