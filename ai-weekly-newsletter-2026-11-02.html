Anthropic · S-1

Anthropic's IPO in 10 numbers

Anthropic filed its S-1 IPO prospectus. It's the company behind the AI tool most of our clients use every day. AP reported the raise could reach $100bn.

Big Tech

Big Tech sells off as AI spending outruns cash flow

Alphabet's cloud revenue more than doubled, but its search business slowed and free cash flow was negative $9.1bn, Fortune reported. Its shares then lost about $500bn of market value in a day, their worst day on record (TechCrunch). S&P cut Meta's outlook to negative on AI spending, and across Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle, capex has passed operating cash flow for the first time in data going back to 2011 (24/7 Wall St). The cost of insuring their debt against default is at its highest in years (WSJ), and for Oracle it has more than doubled since January (FT).

Why it mattersAI revenue is growing fast, but legacy businesses like search are slowing while they still pay for the build-out. More of it now comes through debt, and our clients are the lenders.

Regulators

Bank of England and FCA: 80% of UK firms run AI agents, 2% let them act alone

Their survey of 196 firms found 86% use AI, up from 75% in 2024. 80% have agents deployed, but only 2% give them broad autonomy. Third parties now provide 53% of the AI systems firms rate most important, up from 35%.

Why it mattersAgents are now in most firms, but people still sign off on what they produce. So the data the agent reads has to be right and traceable, which is exactly what we sell.

Platforms

Databricks maps out a KYC agent for client onboarding

Databricks set out an onboarding workflow where a supervisor agent hands work to specialist agents for documents, KYC and screening, and risk, and people keep the final decision. It cites a 2024 EY study: more than half of the asset managers surveyed involve at least 10 teams in onboarding.

Why it mattersThe KYC and screening agent needs entity, ownership and sanctions data, and Databricks doesn't name a provider. That's the gap our KYC data fills.

ICE · Q3 call

ICE: clients pay for the data, not the compute

On the Q3 call of ICE (the exchange group, also a big fixed income data provider), president Ben Jackson said "Clients pay for the value of the data, not the compute, so our economics are tied to the data, not to which model wins." He also said hyperscalers have started buying ICE data subscriptions directly and setting up trading desks, which he linked to the power their data centres need.

Why it mattersThe data argument is ours too. And the hyperscalers now trade energy to secure power for AI, so they are becoming data clients in their own right.

What asset managers are doing

Private credit

Alternative managers split on AI chip risk

Blackstone (Anthropic) and Apollo (SpaceX) are lending against AI chips, but others are more careful. Ares said on its call it does not know how to underwrite how fast chips lose value, so it sticks to data centres pre-leased to hyperscalers. Blue Owl co-CEO Marc Lipschultz said "Chips have a lot of technology risk" (call), and Oaktree co-CEO Armen Panossian is "very, very cautious" on data centre deals, Bloomberg reported.

Why it mattersChips and buildings are different credits, and telling them apart needs data on the borrower, the lease and who stands behind it.

Systematic

BlackRock's systematic team builds a machine analyst

Mike Pyle, BlackRock's deputy head of portfolio management, told Hedgeweek that its systematic team has been "building a platform that replicates from beginning to end much of what a fundamental equity analyst can do".

Why it mattersSystematic equity teams tend to move ahead of credit teams, so expect systematic credit firms to follow.

Traditional managers

Amundi's GenAI model halves analysis time

Amundi, Europe's largest asset manager, said its Investment Institute built a model combining its cross-asset framework with AI to build scenarios and allocation proposals. Amundi says it saves half the analysis time compared with previous tools.

Why it mattersTraditional managers are now reporting AI time savings in their results.

Concept of the week

Model routing

ICE ties its economics to the data, not to which model wins (see above), and model routing is a big reason why. A model router analyses each prompt in real time and sends it to the most suitable model: a fast, cheap one for easy tasks, a frontier model for the ones that need deeper reasoning.

Think of a multi-manager platform, which sends each trade to the pod best placed to run it: a router sends each task to the model best placed to answer it. For us it means the same client question can be answered by a Claude model one day and by a ChatGPT one the next, so our data has to work under every model.

You'll hear this from clients when they say they don't want to be locked into one model, or ask which models our MCP works with.
